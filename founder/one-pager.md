# Cyra Health: one page

**Verdict: Not yet.** The unit economics work. Demand isn't proven yet: 0 of 20 simulated buyers paid in month one.

**The problem.** Women with PCOS have irregular cycles that mainstream period apps mispredict. Between visits they forget what happened, and they feel brushed off at appointments. The privacy of period apps is under public scrutiny: the Flo/Meta jury verdict in 2025 (`competitors.md`).

**The customer.** Women in the US aged 18–40 with diagnosed or suspected PCOS, starting with those who have an appointment coming up.

**The offer: Cyra Appointment-Ready.**
- A one-page summary for the clinician, built on the phone: cycles, symptoms, what changed since starting treatment, and guideline-cited questions to ask.
- A 3-tap daily log and a first-week baseline.
- No account, no ads, no tracking. Weight is off by default. AI is optional and previewed before anything is sent.
- **The first summary is free.** After that, $7.99 per appointment, or Pro at $3.99 a month or $29.99 a year.
- Founding members pay $19.99 a year.

The break-even assumes the founder takes no pay. At $3,000 a month of founder pay, break-even rises to 16 packs a day.

**The evidence**
- **Buyer panel (simulated):**
  - 0 of 20 paid in month one.
  - On the Appointment-Ready offer, 18 of 20 named a specific trigger: "$7.99 before my next appointment, once the free summary helps".
- **Competitors:**
  - 20 verified. Annual Pro prices run $29.99–$129.99, with a median of $54.99.
  - The gap: PCOS-first, a free doctor summary, and data that stays on the device. No one combines all three.
  - Apple Health already covers part of this for free on iPhone.
- **Real tests:** none yet. A waitlist price test runs 9–29 Nov 2026, then a 6-week pilot with 20–30 real users.

**What the money is for: $4,362**
- Clinician review of the medical wording: about $1,500 (estimate).
- Legal review of the privacy policy and terms: about $2,500 (estimate).
- The price test: $300.
- App store fees.

The product itself is already built and tested.

**The ask.** Fund the clinician and legal reviews and the pilot. Hold any growth spending until the pilot shows at least 5 real payments and at least 30% of users saying the summary changed their appointment.

**Biggest open issue.** An app called "Cyra – PCOS & Cycle Tracker" already exists in the App Store, so the name needs a lawyer's check, and likely a new name, before launch.

*The buyers are simulated and every number is a projection. Real customers and real quotes confirm them. Not financial, legal or tax advice.*
