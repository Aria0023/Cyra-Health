# Product lens
Score: 6 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **A real moment to design around.** A woman with irregular cycles walks into a clinician's office with a clear, honest record instead of memory. The free doctor-ready PDF is that moment, and it is free. That is the right instinct.
- **Restraint already shows.** No streaks, scores, grades or completeness bars. Food tracking left out. Insights framed as "associations to raise with your clinician". These are choices to say no, and they make the experience calmer.
- **Privacy runs through the whole experience, not just one feature.** Local-first storage, guest use, no ads or third-party analytics, a previewed AI payload, and encrypted backup are all part of one stance. Few competitors can claim that end to end.
- **Care in the parts nobody sees.** Every medical sentence cites a guideline, pending clinician sign-off. The product is built and tested before launch.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** The brief describes a PCOS app, plus five life stages, POTS, hair loss planned, add-on verticals, and an aim to "appeal to women with different problems". One life stage per user helps inside the app. It does not fix a company that is building several products at once with one founder. The pitch cannot be said in one sentence yet, and the product is not clear yet either.
2. **The daily experience may ask too much for what it gives back.** About 12 taps a day, across five tabs and a log sheet, before she has a reason to come back. Irregular cycles mean patterns take months to appear. If the first weeks feel like data entry with no payoff, she leaves before the PDF ever matters. The first five minutes are not described anywhere.
3. **Seams the founder does not yet own.** Clinician review of the wording, legal review, store accounts and billing, and the backend API key are all unfinished. So is how anyone finds the app. Each one is a handoff where the calm experience can break: a rejected store review, an AI feature that fails, a paywall that feels out of place in a "free forever" app. It is also unclear what Pro adds that she *feels*, given how generous the free core is.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, for a woman with suspected PCOS, what does Cyra do?
- Walk me through her first five minutes and her first appointment. What does she see on day 1, day 14, and in the exam room?
- What would you cut if you could only ship the PCOS experience? Would POTS, the other life stages and the hair-loss plans wait?
- What is the smallest daily log that still produces a useful PDF? Could it be three taps?
- What is the one Pro moment she would pay for? What does it cost to deliver per user? (Open question: /founder-cfo. The price itself: /founder-pricing.)
- Do women with PCOS want this enough to log for months, and how will the first ones find it? (Open questions: /founder-consumer for demand, /founder-competitors for what they use today.)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Launch as a PCOS product only. Hide or remove the other life stages and POTS from the store listing and onboarding until PCOS has users who stay.
2. Write the one-sentence pitch and a scripted first five minutes. Show that a new user gets something of value, such as a first pattern or a draft PDF, in her first session.
3. Cut the default daily log to the fewest taps that still feed the PDF, and make everything else optional.
4. Close the seams before store submission: clinician sign-off, legal sign-off, store billing tested end to end, and the AI path working with a clear fallback when it fails.
5. Name the single Pro benefit, and test it with /founder-consumer before setting a price.
